# LearnLanka — Ambiguity Hunt Log

## Brief Reference

LearnLanka is a tutor marketplace platform designed to connect students with qualified tutors for O/L and A/L exam preparation. Students should be able to search tutors by subject, grade, language (Sinhala, Tamil, English), and price range.

Students can book one-hour tutoring sessions and pay through card payments or eZ Cash. Tutors can publish available time slots, accept or decline bookings, and cancel sessions with at least 12 hours notice.

The platform charges a 15% commission from every completed session and pays tutors weekly through bank transfers. After each session, both students and tutors can rate each other from 1–5 stars and leave a short comment.

# Top 3 Questions for the Founders

## 1. What exactly defines a completed session?

This affects commission calculation, tutor payouts, and dispute handling.

Clarification needed:
- Does completion require both users joining?
- Is there a minimum attendance duration?
- Is completion automatic or manually confirmed?

---

## 2. What are the cancellation and refund rules for students and tutors?

The brief only defines tutor cancellation rules.

Clarification needed:
- Can students cancel bookings?
- Are refunds available?
- Are penalties applied for late cancellations?

---

## 3. What verification process is required for tutors?

The system needs a clear definition of what makes a tutor qualified.

Clarification needed:
- Are certificates required?
- Is identity verification needed?
- Does an administrator approve tutors?

# Reflection

## What ambiguity caused the most difficulty?

The biggest challenge was identifying **definition-based ambiguities**. Terms such as "completed session" and "qualified tutor" appear clear but require specific business rules before development can begin.

If these definitions are not clarified, different developers may implement different interpretations, causing inconsistent system behaviour.

---

## Which ambiguity could affect the architecture the most?

The definition of a completed session would have the largest architectural impact.

If completion depends on verified attendance and minimum session duration, the system would need integration with the video provider, session tracking, and automated payment triggers.

If a booking alone represents completion, the system would require much simpler logic.

Therefore, this requirement should be clarified before designing the payment, database, and session management systems.
